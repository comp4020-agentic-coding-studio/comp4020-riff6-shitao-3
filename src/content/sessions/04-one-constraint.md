## Before the studio

Come with a guess at your constraint, not a final answer. A studio's worth of
other people's constraints is the fastest way to find out whether yours is
the interesting one.

## In the studio

Pick exactly one rule and hold it: one continuous line that never lifts, one
colour, a resource that runs out and has to be earned back. The constraint has
to cost you something real --- if removing it wouldn't change how the
instrument feels to use, it isn't a constraint, it's decoration. Build it in,
then build a toggle that turns it off, purely so you can show the room the
difference in the same two minutes.

## Afterwards

Keep the toggle even after the studio. It's the fastest way to answer "why
does this rule matter" for the rest of the semester, including to yourself.

## Stretch goal

Try holding a second, incompatible constraint alongside the first for five
minutes, purely as a throwaway branch you'll delete. It's the fastest way to
feel why one well-chosen constraint beats two half-held ones, rather than
just being told so.
